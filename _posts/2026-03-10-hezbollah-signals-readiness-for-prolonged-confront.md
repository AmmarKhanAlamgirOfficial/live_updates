<div>As rocket fire from Hezbollah intensifies, Israeli officials increasingly acknowledge that the Lebanese resistance movement may be preparing for a sustained campaign along the northern front. According to a source cited by The Jerusalem Post, Israeli assessments suggest that the group’s operations are not a short-term escalation but part of a longer confrontation aimed at exhausting Israeli forces and reshaping the battlefield dynamics along the border.</div><div><br></div><div>Over recent days, Israeli sites in the north have remained under persistent pressure. Anti-tank missiles have repeatedly targeted Israeli forces’ positions near the frontier, reinforcing the sense within Israeli military and diplomatic circles that Hezbollah is deliberately maintaining a steady tempo of attacks. Some Israeli officials have questioned the rationale behind the campaign, portraying it as irrational—an argument frequently invoked by Tel Aviv whenever resistance movements challenge Israel's military posture.</div><div><br></div><div>Meanwhile, Lebanese officials say they are pursuing so-called 'diplomatic channels' to prevent further escalation. Washington, however, is reportedly dissatisfied with the approach of the Lebanese government, accusing them of failing to curb Hezbollah’s actions. According to the same source, the US administration has effectively dismissed the Lebanese proposal for now, and Hezbollah has shown no signs that they will pull back on their resistance operations.</div>
